Eight Weeks to Launch

A professor is moving a residential worship course into the hybrid M.Div. You have eight weeks. Drag the work onto the timeline so nobody is overloaded, then run the build week by week as real problems come up, and send the professor a status update you could actually use.

Designed by Keith Pace, D.M.E. as a sample for Candler's Office of Digital Learning

Part II · Plan, then run the build

Build the timeline

A first draft is already on the timeline, and it has problems. Drag a bar to move a task and drag its right edge to make it longer or shorter. Spreading a task over more weeks lowers the hours it needs each week. With a keyboard, focus a bar and press the left or right arrow to move it; hold Shift with the arrows to change its length. The workload rows at the bottom turn red when someone is overbooked.

Part III · Communicate

Send your faculty partner a weekly update

The draft writes itself from your timeline, so it always shows the current plan. Edit it in your own voice. A good update is short, honest about risk, and tells the faculty partner exactly what you need from them.

Why this activity

  • It mirrors the job. Candler's Office of Digital Learning partners with faculty to build Canvas courses for the hybrid M.Div. and online D.Min. This walks through that work: scoping, scheduling around a professor's real calendar, booking Studio 250, and protecting accessibility.
  • Real tradeoffs, not a quiz. Every choice moves hours between people and weeks. Some quick fixes, such as launching with auto-captions, look efficient but break accessibility law and are flagged.
  • Uses beyond hiring. The same planner works as a faculty onboarding tool for anyone new to building online courses, or as a project-management exercise for D.Min. students leading ministry projects.
  • Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA): every drag has a keyboard equivalent, problems are listed in text and not only in color, and the timeline scrolls inside its own frame on small screens.
